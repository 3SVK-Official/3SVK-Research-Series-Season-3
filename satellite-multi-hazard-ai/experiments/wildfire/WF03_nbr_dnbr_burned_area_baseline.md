## 2. Objective

The objective of WF03 is to investigate whether comparing Sentinel-2 imagery acquired before and after a documented wildfire reveals spectral changes consistent with burned areas.

The experiment establishes a reproducible spectral-index baseline, explores the sensitivity of candidate changed-area estimates to different dNBR thresholds, and documents the limitations that must be considered before interpreting the results as burned land.

This experiment complements WF01 and WF02, which focus on active-fire data inspection and the reliability of historical FIRMS detections.

## 3. Research Questions

1. Can NBR reveal spectral differences between pre-fire and post-fire satellite observations?
2. How does dNBR vary across the selected study area?
3. How does the estimated candidate changed area vary with different dNBR thresholds?
4. How do scene selection, cloud masking, acquisition timing, and valid-pixel coverage affect interpretation?
5. What additional independent reference data are required to evaluate burned-area detection performance?

## 4. Data Sources and Selection

### 4.1 Sentinel-2 imagery

The experiment searches the Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC catalogue for the `sentinel-2-l2a` collection.

Catalogue endpoint:

`https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1`

The notebook selects candidate scenes from separate pre-fire and post-fire date windows and initially ranks them by scene-wide cloud-cover percentage.

### 4.2 Spectral bands

| Band | Description                 | Role                                |
| ---- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| B08  | Near-infrared (NIR)         | Input to NBR                        |
| B12  | Short-wave infrared (SWIR2) | Input to NBR                        |
| SCL  | Scene Classification Layer  | Excludes selected unsuitable pixels |

The bands are placed on a common 20 m grid for pixel-wise comparison. The selected scene identifiers, acquisition timestamps, and cloud-cover metadata should be preserved with the experiment results.

### 4.3 Study area and dates

The pilot event is the August 2023 Lahaina wildfire. The notebook uses an approximate geographic bounding box around Lahaina rather than an official fire perimeter.

The initial search windows are:

* Pre-fire: 15 July 2023 to 7 August 2023.
* Post-fire: 10 August 2023 to 15 September 2023.

The actual acquisition dates must be taken from the selected scene metadata generated by the notebook. The search windows are not themselves proof that suitable imagery was found.

## 5. Methodology

### Step 1 — Catalogue search

Search for Sentinel-2 Level-2A scenes intersecting the pilot bounding box and the specified date windows. Record the candidate scenes and select a scene for each period.

### Step 2 — Band loading and alignment

Load B08, B12, and SCL for both selected scenes. Place the bands on a common grid and verify that the pre-fire and post-fire arrays have matching shapes.

Matching array shapes alone do not prove correct spatial alignment; the spatial coordinates and transforms should also be checked.

### Step 3 — Pixel quality filtering

Construct a validity mask from finite spectral values and the selected SCL classes. For this baseline, the notebook retains SCL classes 2, 4, 5, and 6, excluding other classes.

A pixel is used in the comparison only when it passes the validity checks on both dates. Record the number and percentage of common valid pixels.

### Step 4 — Calculate NBR

NBR is calculated using:

$$
NBR=\frac{B08-B12}{B08+B12}
$$

Pixels with a zero or invalid denominator are excluded. NBR values are masked wherever the shared validity mask is false.

### Step 5 — Calculate dNBR

The differenced Normalized Burn Ratio is calculated as:

$$
dNBR=NBR_{\text{pre-fire}}-NBR_{\text{post-fire}}
$$

Positive dNBR values represent locations where NBR decreased between the selected observations. Such changes can be consistent with fire-related surface changes, but they are not uniquely caused by fire.

### Step 6 — Threshold sensitivity analysis

The notebook compares exploratory thresholds of 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, and 0.40.

For each threshold, it records:

* Number of valid pixels.
* Number of candidate changed pixels.
* Percentage of valid pixels selected.
* Approximate candidate area in square kilometres.

The candidate area is calculated from the approximate 20 m × 20 m output grid. It should not be described as confirmed burned area without independent validation.

### Step 7 — Visualization and export

The experiment exports pre-fire NBR, post-fire NBR, dNBR, candidate-change maps, threshold-sensitivity results, and summary plots where the corresponding notebook cells complete successfully.

### Step 8 — Quality checks and artifact verification

Automated checks assess valid-pixel availability, temporal ordering, threshold-table completeness, and whether candidate area is non-increasing as the threshold increases.

The output audit records whether expected files exist and are non-empty. These checks establish computational consistency, not scientific accuracy.

## 8. Limitations

1. **Pilot area limitation:** The selected bounding box is not an official wildfire perimeter and may contain burned and unburned land.
2. **Cloud filtering:** Scene-wide cloud cover does not guarantee cloud-free pixels inside the study area.
3. **Masking errors:** The Scene Classification Layer can misclassify pixels and may not remove every cloud or shadow.
4. **Temporal differences:** Different acquisition dates can introduce changes unrelated to fire.
5. **Spectral ambiguity:** Urban surfaces, bare soil, vegetation changes, water, smoke, and other conditions can affect NBR and dNBR.
6. **Threshold dependence:** Candidate area changes according to the selected threshold.
7. **Resolution:** The analysis uses a common 20 m grid; small or narrow features may be missed or mixed within pixels.
8. **No independent ground truth:** The experiment does not establish burned-area accuracy without a separate reference perimeter or validated burned-area dataset.
9. **No model-performance claim:** This is a spectral-index baseline, not a trained machine-learning model. Accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and IoU are not established by threshold sensitivity alone.

## 10. Conclusion

WF03 establishes a reproducible baseline for comparing pre-fire and post-fire Sentinel-2 spectral observations using NBR and dNBR. It examines how candidate spectral-change estimates vary with exploratory thresholds and records the data-quality limitations that affect interpretation.

The experiment can help identify locations requiring further investigation, but it does not by itself establish a verified burn perimeter or quantify burned-area detection accuracy. Independent reference data and a documented validation procedure are required before making those claims.
