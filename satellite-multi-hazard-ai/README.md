# Satellite-Based Multi-Hazard Intelligence System

## Overview

This project investigates the use of satellite and environmental
observations for monitoring three types of environmental hazards:

- Floods
- Forest disturbances
- Wildfires

The project focuses on understanding whether complementary observations
from different satellite sensors and environmental datasets can improve
the reliability of hazard detection and assessment.

The work follows a research-oriented workflow:

1. Study existing systems and scientific literature.
2. Identify documented limitations and failure cases.
3. Define research hypotheses.
4. Reproduce selected baseline approaches.
5. Conduct controlled experiments.
6. Evaluate results using measurable metrics.
7. Analyze false positives and false negatives.
8. Develop and document the resulting framework.

## Central Research Question

Can combining complementary satellite and environmental observations
reduce missed detections and false alarms compared with relying on
individual observations for flood, forest-disturbance, and wildfire
monitoring?

## Research Scope

### Flood

The flood track will investigate satellite-based flood inundation
detection and contextual information such as rainfall and terrain.

Potential observations include:

- Sentinel-1 SAR
- Sentinel-2 optical imagery
- Rainfall datasets
- Digital Elevation Models

### Forest Disturbance

The forest track will investigate satellite-based detection of changes
in forest and vegetation cover.

Potential observations include:

- Sentinel-1 SAR
- Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery
- Landsat imagery

### Wildfire

The wildfire track will investigate active-fire detection and
post-fire assessment.

Potential observations include:

- VIIRS active-fire observations
- MODIS fire products
- Sentinel-2 imagery
- Environmental and weather information

## Important Research Boundary

This project does not assume that satellite observations can perfectly
predict all disasters before they occur.

Different hazards require different forms of analysis:

- Floods: risk/context assessment and inundation detection
- Forests: disturbance/change detection
- Wildfires: fire-risk assessment, active-fire detection and
  burned-area assessment

The final research contribution will be determined through literature
review, baseline experiments, validation and error analysis.

## Research Status

The project is currently in the research and baseline-study stage.

No performance claims are made at this stage.

## Initial Data and Technology Sources

The project will investigate publicly available Earth-observation
and environmental datasets from organizations including:

- European Space Agency (ESA)
- Copernicus
- NASA
- Global Forest Watch
- Other documented scientific data providers
