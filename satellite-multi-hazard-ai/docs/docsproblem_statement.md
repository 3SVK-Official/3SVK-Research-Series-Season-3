# Problem Statement

## Background

Satellite Earth-observation data are widely used to monitor changes
in the Earth's surface and environment.

Different satellite sensors observe different characteristics of the
Earth.

For example, Sentinel-1 uses synthetic aperture radar (SAR) to provide
Earth-surface imagery during both day and night and under many weather
conditions. Sentinel-2 uses multispectral optical observations with
13 spectral bands and is widely used for vegetation, land-cover and
disaster-related applications.

For wildfire monitoring, NASA's Fire Information for Resource
Management System (FIRMS) provides active-fire and thermal-anomaly
observations from sensors including MODIS and VIIRS.

These different observations provide complementary information, but
they also have different limitations.

## Problem

A single satellite observation may not always provide sufficient
information for reliable hazard assessment.

Potential limitations include:

- Cloud or atmospheric obstruction in optical imagery.
- Ambiguous signals in satellite observations.
- Missed events.
- False detections.
- Differences in spatial and temporal resolution.
- Limited contextual information.
- Difficulty distinguishing natural changes from human-caused
  disturbances.
- Differences between what a satellite detects and what is occurring
  on the ground.

For example, NASA FIRMS explicitly notes that satellite-derived
active-fire and thermal-anomaly detections have limited accuracy,
that thermal anomalies may originate from sources other than fire,
and that cloud cover can obscure detections.

Similarly, current forest-monitoring systems already use multiple
optical and radar-based alert sources, demonstrating that different
observation systems can provide complementary information.

## Research Problem

The research problem addressed by this project is:

> How can complementary satellite and environmental observations be
> evaluated and combined to improve the reliability of monitoring
> floods, forest disturbances and wildfires?

## Research Question

Can combining complementary satellite and environmental observations
reduce missed detections and false alarms compared with relying on
individual observations?

## Scope

The project will initially investigate three hazard categories:

1. Floods
2. Forest disturbances
3. Wildfires

Each hazard will be studied separately because the physical signals,
data sources, detection objectives and evaluation methods are different.

## Research Approach

The project will:

1. Review existing scientific and operational systems.
2. Identify documented limitations.
3. Select representative historical events and datasets.
4. Reproduce selected baseline approaches.
5. Test complementary observations.
6. Measure performance using appropriate evaluation metrics.
7. Analyze false positives, false negatives and other failure cases.
8. Document the resulting findings and limitations.

## Current Status

The research problem has been defined, but the final research gap and
technical contribution have not yet been established.

The research gap will be determined from literature review and
experimental evidence rather than assumed in advance.
