Limitations

The proposed research has several limitations that may affect the interpretation and generalization of the experimental results.

1. Reference Data Quality and Availability
   The evaluation depends on the availability and reliability of reference or ground-truth data. Incomplete, inconsistent, or inaccurate reference information may affect the measured performance of the monitoring systems.

2. Limited Historical Events and Geographic Coverage
   The number of suitable historical events and study areas may be limited by the availability of satellite observations and reference data. Therefore, the results may not fully represent all geographic regions or environmental events.

3. Missing and Poor-Quality Observations
   Satellite observations may be unavailable or degraded because of cloud cover, acquisition gaps, sensor limitations, or other data-quality issues. These limitations may affect detection performance and the comparison between different configurations.

4. Differences in Spatial and Temporal Resolution
   The datasets used in the study have different spatial and temporal resolutions. Spatial and temporal alignment may therefore introduce information loss, uncertainty, or differences in the level of detail available from each observation source.

5. Sensor-Specific Limitations
   Each satellite and environmental data source has its own limitations. Optical observations can be affected by cloud cover, SAR observations may produce ambiguous responses, and thermal observations may contain anomalies that are not related to the target hazard.

6. Difficulty in Identifying the Cause of Errors
   False positives and false negatives may result from multiple factors, including sensor limitations, environmental conditions, preprocessing errors, temporal mismatches, or reference-data limitations. Therefore, identifying a single cause for every failure may not always be possible.

7. Limited Sample Size for Statistical Analysis
   If the number of independent events or samples is limited, statistical comparisons may have reduced reliability. In such cases, the results will be treated as exploratory rather than being used to make broad generalizations.

8. Hazard-Specific Behavior
   The usefulness of complementary information may differ between flood, forest-disturbance, and wildfire monitoring. Therefore, findings from one hazard cannot automatically be generalized to the other hazards.

9. Computational and Processing Complexity
   Multi-source monitoring requires data collection, quality control, spatial and temporal preprocessing, feature generation, and comparative evaluation across multiple datasets. This increases the computational and implementation requirements of the research.

10. Generalization to Unseen Conditions
    Even when independent events or geographic regions are used for validation, unusual environmental conditions that are not represented in the experimental datasets may produce different results.

These limitations will be documented alongside the experimental results so that the findings can be interpreted within the scope of the available data, study areas, observation conditions, and experimental design.
